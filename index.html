Herd & Homeland

Animals, Ethics and Hard Choices in Wartime Sweden

A research game about the hard choices war forces on us concerning animals — how the welfare of animals is weighed against human safety, livelihoods and the need for food, and what we owe animals when normal care is no longer possible.

About the Project

What is Herd & Homeland?

War confronts society with difficult ethical choices concerning animals. When feed, veterinary care and labour run short, animal keepers, authorities and citizens are forced into decisions without clear answers — decisions that peacetime preparedness planning has largely left unanswered.

Herd & Homeland is a research project developing a serious computer game about these choices. In the game, players act as ethical advisors in a crisis command room, weighing the competing claims of the people around the table as war disrupts normal animal care. The project brings together animal welfare science, ethics and game development.

The project is funded by Formas, the Swedish Research Council for Sustainable Development, and runs from November 2026 to October 2028.

Project Aim

To give the protection and welfare of animals a more natural place in war preparedness — by building a game that lets us face these choices, and talk about them, before they happen for real.

1

Find the dilemmas

Identify the hard choices that arise when war strikes animal care — together with the people who would face them.

2

Build the game

Turn the dilemmas into a game where players must weigh competing claims and make decisions that all come at a cost.

3

Start the conversation

Show how different people choose and why — and open a democratic conversation about what we owe animals in war.

The Game

An ethical advisor in the crisis room

War has broken out and normal life has stopped. Feed, veterinary care and labour are running short. Around the table sit people who represent different interests — and they all need an answer from you.

  • Listen to the table

    Farmers, veterinarians, authorities and animal welfare organisations give their perspectives as the dilemmas unfold.

  • Call witnesses, ask questions

    Dig deeper before you decide. Every claim can be questioned and weighed against the others.

  • Decide — at a cost

    The situations grow harder as the war goes on. There are no clean answers, and no decision is without cost.

  • See how others chose

    Afterwards you can compare your choices with how other players — and professionals — reasoned.

Research

What the game will show

Herd & Homeland is both a game and a research instrument. Behind it lies a systematic mapping of the dilemmas that arise when war disrupts animal care — and when the game is played, it captures how people reason about them.

The dilemmas

The project maps the ethical dilemmas concerning animal care in war, based on previous research and Swedish regulations and preparedness plans, and tests them in interviews and workshops with people working in animal care, animal protection and crisis preparedness. What comes out of the game

How people prioritise

The game records the decisions players make and how they weigh animal welfare against other values. This lets us compare how professionals in war preparedness and citizens reason — and how priorities shift as the war goes on.

Open data

The game and its anonymised data will be made freely available in line with the FAIR principles. Other researchers can build on the platform with new dilemmas, more animals, other crises and other countries.

A basis for policy and preparedness

By showing how different actors reason and why, the results give decision-makers and authorities a basis for including animal protection and welfare in war preparedness.

A tool for education and conversation

Students and professionals can meet the dilemmas before they face them for real, and the visualised results open a democratic conversation about what we owe animals in war.

Research Phases

Phase 1 Current
Nov 2026 – Mar 2027

Mapping the Dilemmas

A review of research on animal welfare, animal ethics and animal protection during war, and of Swedish regulations and preparedness plans.

Phase 2
Mar – Aug 2027

Interviews & Workshops

Interviews and focus groups with practitioners in animal welfare and war preparedness validate the dilemmas and turn them into realistic scenarios.

Phase 3
Apr 2027 – Feb 2028

Game Development & Playtesting

The scenarios are built into the game through scenario writing, design, development and playtesting until it is ready for data collection.

Phase 4
Feb – Oct 2028

Game Release & Data Collection

The game is released to the public and to the groups concerned. The data are analysed and the results shared openly, together with visualisations of how players reasoned.

Presentations, publications and research data will be published here as the project progresses.
