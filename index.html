Herd & Homeland

Animals, Ethics and Hard Choices in Wartime Sweden

A serious game and research project on what we owe animals when war disrupts normal care — and how professionals and citizens reason when the welfare of animals collides with human safety, economic survival and the duty to produce food.

About the Project

What is Herd & Homeland?

Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine has exposed ethical dilemmas in animal care that peacetime routines do not prepare us for. When feed, veterinary care and labour run short, animal keepers are forced into decisions without clear answers: continuing to care for the animals may put people at risk, the absence of veterinary care may prolong suffering that cannot be relieved, and a shortage of feed may force a choice about which animals can be kept alive at all.

Yet research on animals during war has focused almost exclusively on keeping food production running, treating animals as production capacity rather than as sentient beings whose welfare becomes the object of genuine moral conflict. This two-year project provides the first systematic mapping of the ethical dilemmas concerning animal care that arise when war disrupts normal conditions, and investigates how professionals and citizens reason about them.

At the heart of the project is Herd & Homeland, a serious computer game in which the player acts as an ethical advisor in a crisis command room. Around the table sit stakeholders who represent different interests and present their perspectives as the dilemmas unfold. The player can call in witnesses, ask follow-up questions and weigh their claims against each other before a decision must finally be made — and no decision is without cost.

The project is funded by Formas, the Swedish Research Council for Sustainable Development.

Research Questions

  1. What ethical dilemmas concerning animal welfare arise when war disrupts normal animal care?
  2. How do key stakeholders in war preparedness prioritize between different values in ethical dilemmas concerning animal welfare?
  3. How do citizens prioritize between different values in ethical dilemmas concerning animal welfare?

The Full Breadth of Society's Animals

The dilemmas in the game span every kind of animal a society keeps — from the smallest companion animals to facilities with tens of thousands of animals.

Production animals

Cattle, pigs, poultry and sheep — and invertebrates such as insects.

Companion & hobby animals

Dogs, cats, horses and the animals that share our homes.

Zoos & shelters

Animals in zoos, wildlife parks and animal shelters.

Service & working animals

Military, police, rescue and assistance animals.

Semi-domesticated animals

Animals such as reindeer and ostriches.

Research Phases

Phase 1 Current
Nov 2026 – Mar 2027

Mapping the Dilemmas

A structured review of research on animal welfare, animal ethics and animal protection during war, together with Swedish legal and preparedness frameworks.

Phase 2
Mar – Aug 2027

Workshops with Stakeholders

Interviews and focus groups with practitioners in animal welfare and war preparedness validate and refine the dilemmas into realistic scenarios.

Phase 3
Apr 2027 – Feb 2028

Building the Game

The validated scenarios become the serious game through scenario scripting, design, build and playtesting.

Phase 4
Feb – Oct 2028

Release & Data Collection

The game is released openly to the public and to stakeholder groups, collecting data on how professionals and citizens reason about the dilemmas.

Research

An Open and Lasting Research Platform

The project unites three knowledge traditions that rarely meet: animal welfare science, on what animals need; ethics and politics, on how competing obligations should be weighed; and game development, as a method for capturing how people reason about these dilemmas.

Three Functions of the Game

A pedagogical tool that lets stakeholders in war preparedness confront these dilemmas before they arise in practice.

A research instrument that captures the decisions players make.

A space for democratic deliberation that makes visible how various stakeholders reasoned and why.

Ethical Humility

By placing players inside a situation where they must weigh the competing claims of others before deciding, the game aims to foster ethical humility: the capacity to take seriously perspectives unlike one's own, even where deep disagreement remains.

Open Science

The game is released open access, freely playable by anyone. Its scenarios and the anonymised data it generates will be shared in line with the FAIR principles, so that other researchers can build on the platform rather than start from scratch.

Built to Grow

The game is built around Swedish conditions but is general enough to be recognised in other countries, opening the way for comparative studies. New versions can take on new dilemmas, new crises — drought, flood, wildfire, disease outbreak, prolonged power failure — and further kinds of animals.

Publications, presentations, research data and news about the game will be published here as the project progresses.
