Herd & Homeland

Animals, Ethics and Hard Choices in Wartime Sweden

War confronts society with difficult ethical choices concerning animals. In Herd & Homeland we are developing a computer game that will provide new knowledge about how the welfare of animals is weighed against other important values.

The Project

When war sweeps away the conditions for animal care

War sweeps away the conditions for ordinary animal care. When feed, veterinary care and labour are not enough, society faces ethical dilemmas that peacetime preparedness planning has largely left unanswered:

How much risk can we ask staff and volunteers to take to help animals that would otherwise suffer severely?
How long should an animal be kept alive when its suffering, caused by the war, probably cannot be relieved?
Do we have the same moral responsibility for companion animals, farm animals and wild animals during war?

In Herd & Homeland we are developing a computer game that explores how we can relate ethically to questions like these.

All Kinds of Animals

From the home and the farm to the forest

The dilemmas in Herd & Homeland will concern all kinds of animals affected by war — from the single companion animal in the home and the hundreds of animals on the farm to the thousands that live in the forest.

Decisions about animals also affect other things at stake, such as the safety of those who care for the animals, farmers' livelihoods and society's need for food.

The research that exists on animals in war has mainly concerned their role in food supply, where animals are seen as a resource to keep running. Herd & Homeland takes a broader approach and asks what we owe the animals themselves when war makes ordinary care impossible.

  • Farm animals
  • Wild animals
  • Zoos & shelters
  • Service & working animals

The Game

An ethical advisor in the crisis room

In Herd & Homeland the player takes on the role of ethical advisor in a crisis command room. Around the table sit people who represent different interests, and they give their perspectives as the dilemmas unfold.

The player can call in witnesses, ask follow-up questions and weigh their claims against each other before a decision finally has to be made. The situations grow harder as the war goes on — and no decision is without cost.

The game will be free for anyone to play. Afterwards, players can see both their own choices and how others have reasoned.

How the game is made

The project combines animal welfare science, ethics and game development. First, the ethical dilemmas that arise when war strikes animal care are mapped, based on previous research and Swedish regulations and preparedness plans. The dilemmas are then tested and developed in interviews and focus groups with people working in animal care, animal protection and crisis preparedness. The results form the basis of the game.

  1. Nov 2026 – Mar 2027 Now

    Mapping the dilemmas

    Previous research, Swedish regulations and preparedness plans.

  2. Mar – Aug 2027

    Testing with practitioners

    Interviews and focus groups turn the dilemmas into realistic scenarios.

  3. Apr 2027 – Feb 2028

    Building the game

    Scenario writing, design, development and playtesting.

  4. 2028

    Release & conversation

    The game is released to the public and to the groups concerned.

The Conversation

A game that starts a conversation

The game generates several types of data in an accessible format, so it can be used in policy, education and research. Above all, it makes visible how different people reason — and why.

For decision-makers and the public

The game makes visible how different actors reason and why, providing a basis for a democratic conversation about what we owe animals in war.

In education and preparedness

Students and professionals can encounter the dilemmas before they face them for real.

For researchers

The game and its anonymised data become an open infrastructure that can be expanded with new dilemmas, for more animals, in other situations.

By having to weigh the claims of others before deciding, players practise ethical humility — taking seriously perspectives unlike their own, even where disagreement remains.
