Herd & Homeland

Animals, Ethics and Hard Choices in Wartime Sweden

War confronts society with difficult ethical choices concerning animals. Herd & Homeland is a computer game that puts you in the crisis room — and lets us face these choices, and talk about them, before they happen for real.

About the game Share your perspective
Our Aim

A game that gives the protection and welfare of animals a more natural place in war preparedness.

1

Find the dilemmas

Identify the hard choices that arise when war strikes animal care — together with the people who would face them.

2

Build the game

Turn the dilemmas into a game where you must weigh competing claims and make decisions that all come at a cost.

3

Start the conversation

Show how different people choose and why — and open a democratic conversation about what we owe animals in war.

The Game

You are the ethical advisor in the crisis room

War has broken out and normal life has stopped. Feed, veterinary care and labour are running short. Around the table sit people who represent different interests — and they all need an answer from you.

  • Listen to the table

    Farmers, veterinarians, authorities and animal welfare organisations give their perspectives as the dilemmas unfold.

  • Call witnesses, ask questions

    Dig deeper before you decide. Every claim can be questioned and weighed against the others.

  • Decide — at a cost

    The situations grow harder as the war goes on. There are no clean answers, and no decision is without cost.

  • See how others chose

    Afterwards you can compare your choices with how other players — and professionals — reasoned.

Making the Game

Built together with those who would face the choices

The game brings together animal welfare science, ethics and game development. The dilemmas are tested and developed in interviews and workshops with people who work with animal care, animal protection and crisis preparedness — so that what happens in the game is true to reality.

  1. Nov 2026 – Mar 2027 Now

    Finding the dilemmas

    Drawing on previous research and Swedish regulations and preparedness plans.

  2. Mar – Aug 2027

    Testing with practitioners

    Interviews and workshops turn the dilemmas into realistic scenarios.

  3. Apr 2027 – Feb 2028

    Building the game

    Scenario writing, design, development and playtesting.

  4. 2028

    Release & conversation

    The game is released free to the public and to the groups concerned.

The Conversation

A game that starts a conversation

Herd & Homeland is not only played — it is talked about. By making visible how different people choose and why, the game becomes a shared starting point for discussing what we owe animals in war.

For the public and decision-makers

See how farmers, vets, authorities and fellow citizens reason — a basis for a democratic conversation about animals in war.

In education and preparedness exercises

Students and professionals can meet the dilemmas — and each other's perspectives — before they face them for real.

As an open platform

The game and its anonymised data are made openly available, so the platform can grow with new dilemmas, more animals and other crises.

By having to weigh the claims of others before deciding, players practise ethical humility — taking seriously perspectives unlike their own, even where disagreement remains.
